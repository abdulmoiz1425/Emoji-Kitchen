Google Messages is one of the easiest places to send an Emoji Kitchen mashup on Android. Type an emoji, tap a sticker suggestion, and a sticker-style mashup like a heart-eyed ghost lands in your conversation. When it works, it takes a couple of seconds. When it doesn't, there's usually a simple reason.

This guide covers two ways to send mashups in Google Messages: through Gboard's sticker suggestions, and by attaching a mashup image you saved yourself. It also covers the most common problems, like suggestions not showing up or stickers arriving blurry.

What Emoji Kitchen looks like in Google Messages

Emoji Kitchen mashups are images, not new emoji characters. When you send one in Google Messages, it travels as a sticker or picture, not as text. That matters for two reasons:

  • The person you're texting doesn't need Gboard to see it. They receive an image, so it shows up on most phones, including iPhone.
  • It can't be typed inside a sentence the way a normal emoji can. If you need a regular emoji in your text, use your keyboard's emoji tab or our online emoji keyboard.

Method 1: Use Gboard sticker suggestions

This is the built-in way on Android, and it's the fastest.

What you need

  • Gboard installed and set as your active keyboard. Other keyboards, including Samsung Keyboard, don't show Gboard's mashups. If you're on a Galaxy phone, see our Emoji Kitchen on Samsung guide for switching keyboards.
  • Up-to-date apps. Update Gboard and Google Messages from the Play Store if you haven't in a while.

Steps

  1. Open Google Messages and start or open a conversation.
  2. Tap the text box so Gboard opens.
  3. Tap the emoji icon on the keyboard to open the emoji panel.
  4. Tap an emoji, such as 😍 or 👻.
  5. Look for a row of sticker suggestions above the emoji panel. These are the mashups.
  6. Tap a second emoji to see mashups that combine the two, if that pair exists.
  7. Tap the mashup you want. It's added to the conversation, usually ready to send right away.

Not every pair has a mashup. Each one is designed artwork, so some combinations simply don't exist. If nothing appears, try a different emoji.

Method 2: Attach a saved mashup image

Use this method if Gboard suggestions don't appear, if you use a different keyboard, or if you want a specific pair you found online.

  1. Open the Emoji Kitchen Hub mixer in your phone's browser.
  2. Pick two emoji. If that pair has a mashup, it shows in the preview.
  3. Tap the download button to save the image to your phone.
  4. Open Google Messages and go to your conversation.
  5. Tap the attach or + button next to the text box.
  6. Choose your gallery or files, then pick the mashup image.
  7. Send it.

Troubleshooting: Emoji Kitchen not working in Google Messages

No sticker suggestions appear

  • Check your keyboard. Make sure Gboard is the keyboard that's open. On many phones you can switch by tapping the keyboard icon at the bottom of the screen while typing.
  • Update Gboard and Google Messages. Older versions may not show mashups.
  • Check Gboard's emoji and sticker settings. In Gboard settings, look for emoji, sticker, or suggestion options and make sure suggestions are turned on. Exact menu names change between versions.
  • Try a different emoji. Some emoji have few or no mashups.

Still stuck? Our Emoji Kitchen not working guide walks through more fixes.

The mashup pastes as nothing or a broken image

Pasting images into a text box doesn't always work in every app or keyboard version. Instead, save the image and attach it with the + button (Method 2). Attaching a file is usually more reliable than pasting.

The sticker looks blurry or small

How an image looks after sending can depend on how the message is delivered. Picture messages sent as regular MMS may be compressed by your carrier, which can make stickers look soft. Chats that use RCS (often labeled as chat features in Google Messages) generally handle images better. Results vary by carrier, phone, and the other person's app, so there's no single fix, but sending over Wi-Fi with chat features on is worth a try.

The other person sees a plain image, not a sticker

That's normal. Mashups are images, so on the receiving end they may appear as a regular picture, especially on a different phone or messaging app. The artwork itself is the same.

FAQ

Does Google Messages have Emoji Kitchen built in?

Not on its own. The mashup suggestions come from Gboard, Google's keyboard. When you type in Google Messages with Gboard, the keyboard can suggest mashup stickers. With another keyboard, you won't see them, but you can still attach a saved mashup image.

Can the person I text see my Emoji Kitchen sticker if they don't use Android?

Usually, yes. A mashup is sent as an image, so the other person doesn't need Gboard or an Android phone to view it. How it looks can vary with their app and how the message was delivered.

Why can't I find a mashup for two specific emoji?

Each mashup is designed artwork, so not every pair exists. Try a different combination, or check the Emoji Kitchen Hub mixer to preview which pairs have results.

Is Emoji Kitchen Hub the same as Google's Emoji Kitchen?

No. Emoji Kitchen Hub is an unofficial, fan-made website. It is not run by Google and is not affiliated with Google, Gboard, or Google Messages. Google's Emoji Kitchen is a separate feature, and available pairs and options can differ.

Why does my mashup look blurry after I send it?

Image quality can drop when a picture is sent as a standard MMS message, because carriers may compress it. Chats with RCS chat features turned on often keep images sharper, though results depend on your carrier and the other person's phone.

Can I use Emoji Kitchen with Samsung Keyboard in Google Messages?

Samsung Keyboard doesn't show Gboard's mashup suggestions. You can switch to Gboard while typing, or save a mashup from Emoji Kitchen Hub and attach it as an image.
